The probabilities are not equal, but they are not concentrated entirely on one class either.

Why is maximum entropy reached at equal probabilities?

When all three classes have probability 1/3, the model has no preference for any class. The uncertainty is therefore at its maximum.

Hmax = −3 × (1/3) ln(1/3) = ln(3) Approximately 1.0986
Why is minimum entropy reached at [1, 0, 0]?

When one class has probability 1 and the others have probability 0, the model has no uncertainty about its predicted class.

Hmin = −[1 ln(1) + 0 + 0] = 0 Because ln(1) = 0 and 0 ln(0) is defined by its limit as 0
03

Look inside the entropy formula

The formula is not a black box. It performs three simple multiplications and then adds their results.

H = − [ p₁ ln(p₁) + p₂ ln(p₂) + p₃ ln(p₃) ]
Class 1 term
−0.1785
0.80 × ln(0.80)
Class 2 term
−0.2846
0.15 × ln(0.15)
Class 3 term
−0.1498
0.05 × ln(0.05)
H = − [−0.1785 + −0.2846 + −0.1498]
H = 0.6129
Notice that each term is zero when its probability is 0 or 1. Intermediate probabilities contribute positive uncertainty to the total entropy after the minus sign is applied.
Why does p × ln(p) approach zero near p = 0?
ln(p) approaches negative infinity as p approaches zero from the positive side. However, p itself also approaches zero, and the product has this limit:
limp→0⁺ p ln(p) = 0
Therefore, a zero-probability class contributes zero to mathematical entropy. In numerical code, epsilon may be used to avoid evaluating ln(0) directly.
